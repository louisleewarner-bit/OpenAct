# Visual direction

## Subject
A writing tool for stage and screen. The script page is the hero; everything around it is a quiet desk. The visual vocabulary comes from the rehearsal room: the prompt copy's pencil notes in the margin, an actor's highlighter on their lines, and the pale revision-paper colours of a production script.

## Plan
- **Colour:** ink (`#1C2433`) on paper (`#FFFFFF`), on a cool desk grey (`#E4E8EE`); one ink-blue accent (`#2D4DA6`) for actions and focus. Category tints are borrowed from revision paper (blue, pink, goldenrod, salmon) and always paired with a text label. Dark mode keeps the same structure with a dark page, so writers working at night aren't staring at white paper.
- **Type:** Courier Prime for the script (the format demands it, and its metrics are Courier's); Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for everything else, chosen for legibility rather than fashion, which suits principle 7.
- **Layout:** three columns on desktop (outline, page, details) with a status line; two on tablets; one on phones, where the page reflows. An element bar is always on screen: under the page on desktop, above the navigation bar (or the keyboard) on phones (D-038). Text is left-aligned throughout; the only centred text is what the format centres.

## The one bold idea
The margin speaks. The current element's name is written in the page's left margin, in pencil grey with a short ink-blue tick, as a stage manager would annotate a prompt copy. It replaces the boxed focus ring a text editor would normally draw, and it teaches the element names without a tutorial. The highlighter extends the metaphor: choose a character in the Cast panel and their lines are highlighted, like an actor's script.

## Checked against the generic defaults
- Not warm cream with a terracotta accent: the paper is white and the neutrals lean blue, after ink.
- Not near-black with an acid accent: dark mode is a navy page with a soft blue accent.
- No card grid: panels are lists and tables; only the page and dialogs are raised.
- No eyebrow labels in capitals, no middle-dot metadata, no arrows on buttons: keyboard hints use key caps ("Enter Speech").

## Restraint (updated by D-039)
As of D-039 the chrome follows the system in design-system.md: two radii, four text sizes, hairlines instead of boxes, and the accent only for what is current. The wordmark is a spike mark before the name.

One accent, one raised surface (the page), no decorative motion. Radix dialogs fade, and that stops under reduced motion.
