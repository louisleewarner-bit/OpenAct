# User research

## Plan
- **Goal:** confirm or kill the ranked needs in gaps.md, especially the assumptions (casting helpers, phone use, how much panto structure writers want), before building past the core editor.
- **Method:** 6–8 interviews of 30 minutes, then the survey shared in writing and amateur-theatre groups (aim for 100 or more responses).
- **Who:** 3–4 stage writers (panto and musical), 1–2 amateur-theatre directors or stage managers, 2–3 screenwriters (students or beginners).
- **Run by:** the owner (see docs/open-questions.md). Synthesise the notes with design:research-synthesis.

## Interview guide (30 minutes)
**Warm-up (3 min).** Thanks; it's about how you write scripts, not a test; ask to record.

**Context (7 min)**
1. Tell me about the last script you finished. What was it, and who was it for?
2. What did you write it in? Why that?
3. Walk me through getting it from your head to the people who needed it: director, cast, contest, publisher.

**Deep dive (12 min)**
4. What part of formatting takes time or goes wrong? Show me if you can.
5. *Stage writers:* how do you lay out songs, lyrics and stage directions? Who decided that layout?
6. *Panto writers:* how do you plan the running order: front cloths, routines, songsheet, walkdown? Where does that plan live?
7. *Directors:* what do you do with a script once it arrives? Line counts, scene charts, doubling, rehearsal calls: how do you work those out now?
8. *Screenwriters:* what made you pick your tool? What would make you switch?
9. Have you ever lost work, been locked out of a file, or had a format rejected? What happened?
10. How do you feel about AI features in writing tools?
11. Tell me about the last time you sent a script to a competition, theatre or agent. What did you check before sending? Has a script ever been turned away for its format, length or a rule you missed? *(Tests the "Ready to submit?" review, R18.)*

**Reaction (6 min).** Show the prototype. "Write the next line of this scene." Watch where they hesitate; ask what they expected to happen.

**Wrap-up (2 min).** Anything we didn't ask? Can we follow up when there's something to try?

Probes: "Tell me more about that." "What did you do next?" "How often does that happen?"

## Survey (about 5 minutes)
1. What do you write? (Screenplays / TV / Stage plays / Musicals / Pantomime / Radio or audio / Other)
2. Who do you mainly write for? (Myself / Amateur company / Professional theatre / Contests / Film or TV production / Students' coursework)
3. Which tool do you use most? (list from competitors.md, plus Word, Google Docs, Other)
4. What do you pay for it each year? (Nothing / Under £30 / £30–100 / Over £100)
5. Pick up to three things that frustrate you about your current tool. (Price / Script or page limits / Watermarks / Formatting stage or musical scripts / Exporting to Word or PDF / Paper size / Losing work / Needs an account or internet / AI features / Other)
6. How do you send scripts to others? (PDF / Word / Final Draft file / Shared online link / Printed)
7. *If you write musicals or panto:* how do you keep track of songs? (In the script / Separate list / Spreadsheet / Not at all)
8. *If you direct or stage-manage:* which of these do you make by hand? (Line counts / Scene-by-character chart / Doubling plan / Rehearsal scripts / OpenAct lists)
9. Do you write or edit scripts on your phone? (Often / Sometimes / Never)
10. How important is it that your scripts stay on your own device? (1–5)
11. Before you send a script to a competition, theatre or agent, what do you check? (Length / Title page / Removing your name for a blind read / Leftover notes or placeholders / Character names / Formatting / Spelling / Nothing in particular / Other)
12. Which competitions, theatres or schemes do you send work to? (free text)
13. Would you try a free app that handles stage, musical and panto layouts as well as screenplays? (Yes / Maybe / No) Why?
14. Can we contact you to try an early version? (email, optional)

## Proto-personas
Drafts to test, not findings. Each line is marked **[E]** evidence (sources in competitors.md) or **[A]** assumption.

### Pat, the panto writer (primary)
- Writes a full-length family panto each year for an amateur or semi-professional company. [A, based on the owner]
- Works in Word or a template; the director wants A4 PDF and Word. [A]
- Plans the show around front-cloth and full-stage scenes, routines, the songsheet and the walkdown. [E: S19]
- Tracks topical jokes that will date before opening night, and running gags. [A, owner's practice]
- **Job:** when I'm shaping the show, I want to see songs, routines and scene types at a glance, so the running order works and every set change has a front cloth to hide it.

### Morgan, the musical writer
- Writes book and lyrics for a stage musical with a composer. [A]
- Needs songs as first-class script elements: number, singers, dramatic function, lyrics set apart. [E: S15, S18]
- **Job:** when a song spot changes, I want the song list and script to stay in step, so the composer and director always see the current show.

### Sam, the amateur-theatre director
- Receives the script, then casts and schedules rehearsals. [A]
- Works out line counts, who is in which scene, and doubling by hand. [A, no evidence found]
- **Job:** when I cast and plan rehearsals, I want line counts and a scene-by-character chart, so I cast fairly and call only who's needed.

### Jo, the student screenwriter
- Needs correctly formatted pages for coursework and contests on a school laptop or Chromebook. [A]
- Has hit free-tier caps or watermarks. [E: S3, S5, S8]
- **Job:** when I enter a contest, I want a PDF that looks professional without paying, so readers judge my writing, not my software.

## Jobs to be done (shared)
- When I'm drafting, I want the format to take care of itself, so I can stay in the story.
- When I send a draft out, I want the layout and file type the reader expects (A4 or US Letter, PDF or Word), so the format never counts against me.
- When I switch tools or collaborators, I want my scripts in open files, so I'm never locked in.
- When I'm away from my desk, I want to read and fix lines on my phone. [A]
