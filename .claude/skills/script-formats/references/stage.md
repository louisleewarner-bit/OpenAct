# Stage play, musical and pantomime

Stage layout is far less standardised than screenplay layout; even UK guides disagree with each other. So the stage profile ships the defaults below and exposes each difference as a setting. Default to the UK layout on A4 and the US layout on US Letter, and let the writer switch.

## Layouts
- **UK:** the speaker's name at the left in capitals, often bold. By default the speech sits beside the name in a hanging indent (often after a colon); a setting starts it on the line below at the left margin instead. Stage directions in italics, one tab in from the left margin; short directions inside a speech in brackets and italics.
- **US manuscript:** act and scene headings centred; the speaker's name in capitals above the speech, starting about 4 in from the left edge (the Dramatists Guild-recommended guidelines), with centred names as a setting; character names also in capitals inside stage directions; the speech at the left margin; stage directions indented, usually in brackets, italics varying by publisher.
- Options for both: start each scene or act on a new page; page numbers at the top or bottom; act-scene-page numbering (`1-2-15`) or plain page numbers; generous white space for actors' notes.

## Structure and metadata
- Front matter: title page, cast list (each role with a one-line description), setting and time note, scene list, song list.
- Acts and scenes form the outline; each scene can carry a setting ("The palace kitchen").
- Entrances and exits are stage directions tagged with the character, so the app knows who is on stage. A **French scene** starts whenever someone enters or exits; rehearsal schedules often work at that level.
- Character-by-scene chart: a grid of characters against scenes, built from speeches and tagged entrances. It drives rehearsal calls and **doubling** (one actor playing several roles): flag doubled roles that meet in the same scene, or that need a quick change between adjacent scenes.
- Line counts per character, per scene and in total; amateur companies use them for casting.
- Cue layer (after MVP): lighting, sound and music cues, printed in a production copy and hidden in the writer's copy.

## Musicals
- **Song** element: a number (`1`, `2`, `2a` for a reprise, underscore or segue), title and singers, plus notes on dramatic function, staging and musical feel. Songs appear in the outline and in an auto-built song list that includes reprises, underscoring, entr'acte, finale and bows or playout.
- **Lyrics** element: one lyric line per line, set apart from speech. US scripts often set lyrics in capitals, UK scripts in italics or with an indent; make it a profile setting. Singers are named like speakers, including groups such as `ALL` and `ENSEMBLE`.
- A song can be a placeholder (title and function, lyrics to come). For an existing song, the writer names it and describes the staging; the script never needs its words.
- Analysis aids (after MVP): each principal's "I want" moment, songs per character, long stretches without a song.

## Pantomime
Panto is a British stage tradition with its own structure. On top of the stage profile:
- **Scene type** on each scene: full stage, or front cloth (played in front of a drop while the set behind it is changed). Show the pattern in the outline, and gently flag two full-stage scenes in a row, since a set change needs somewhere to happen.
- **Routine** tag for set-piece comedy, such as the ghost "behind you" routine, the slosh scene and the songsheet.
- **Audience participation** tag for call and response ("Oh no it isn't!" / "Oh yes it is!"), booing the villain and "behind you" moments, listed per scene so the balance is easy to check.
- Templates: two acts with alternating full-stage and front-cloth scenes; a songsheet (a front-cloth audience sing-along) and a walkdown finale (the cast take their bows in order, traditionally closing on a rhyming couplet) near the end of Act Two; a cast list seeded with the stock roles: Dame, principal boy, principal girl, comic, villain, good fairy, chorus and juveniles.
- Running-gag and topical-joke tags (after MVP): a topical joke carries a check-before date, such as opening night, and appears in a review list.
